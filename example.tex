\section{Decomposing the Lorentz force}
\label{sec:magnetic-tension}

In ideal MHD, the Lorentz force per unit volume is
\begin{align}
    \mVector{f}_L = \frac{1}{4\pi} \rbrac{\mCurl{\mVector{b}}}\times\mVector{b}
    , \label{eqn:magnetic-tension:lorentz}
\end{align}
where $\mVector{b}$ is the magnetic field. Using the identity $\rbrac{\mCurl{\mVector{b}}}\times\mVector{b} = \rbrac{\mVector{b}\cdot\nabla}\mVector{b} - \mGrad{\rbrac{b^2/2}}$,
\begin{align*}
    \mVector{f}_L
    = \frac{1}{4\pi} \rbrac{\mVector{b}\cdot\nabla}\mVector{b}
        - \mGrad{\rbrac{\frac{b^2}{8\pi}}} ,
\end{align*}
\ie \cref{eqn:magnetic-tension:lorentz} is the divergence of the Maxwell stress tensor,
\begin{align}
    \mVector{f}_L = \mDiv{\mTensor{T}} ,
    \qquad
    \mTensor{T} \equiv \frac{1}{4\pi} \sbrac{\mVector{b}\otimes\mVector{b} - \frac{b^2}{2} \imatrix}
    . \label{eqn:magnetic-tension:stress}
\end{align}

\subsection{Pressure and tension}
\label{sec:magnetic-tension:decomposition}

To see the physical meaning hidden in \S\ref{sec:magnetic-tension}, write $\mVector{b} = b \mVectorUnit{b}$ and split $\rbrac{\mVector{b}\cdot\nabla}\mVector{b}$ along $\mVectorUnit{b}$ and along the principal normal $\mVectorUnit{n}$ of the field lines,
\begin{align}
    \rbrac{\mVector{b}\cdot\nabla}\mVector{b}
    = b \mppFrac{b}{s} \mVectorUnit{b}
        + b^2\kappa \mVectorUnit{n}
    , \label{eqn:magnetic-tension:decomposition}
\end{align}
where $s$ is arc length along the field line and $\kappa \equiv \nbrac{\rbrac{\mVectorUnit{b}\cdot\nabla}\mVectorUnit{b}}$ is the field-line curvature. Substituting \cref{eqn:magnetic-tension:decomposition} into \cref{eqn:magnetic-tension:lorentz} gives
\begin{align}
    \mVector{f}_L
    = -\mGrad{\rbrac{\frac{b^2}{8\pi}}}
        + \frac{b^2\kappa}{4\pi} \mVectorUnit{n}
        + \frac{b}{4\pi} \mppFrac{b}{s} \mVectorUnit{b}
    . \label{eqn:magnetic-tension:final}
\end{align}
The first term of \cref{eqn:magnetic-tension:final} is an isotropic \emph{magnetic pressure} gradient, $p_{\mathrm{mag}} = b^2/8\pi$; the second is the \emph{magnetic tension}, \ie it points along $\mVectorUnit{n}$ and is proportional to the field-line curvature $\kappa$, vanishing for straight field lines. Tension acts like a taut string, pulling bent field lines straight; the ratio of thermal to magnetic pressure is the plasma-$\mPlasmaBeta$, and the Alfv\'en Mach number $\mAlfvenMach$ compares this same tension to inertia.
